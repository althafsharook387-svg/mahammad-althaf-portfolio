Research

MBA dissertation

Capital markets · Event study · 2024–25

Impact of IPO Fund Allocation on Equity Prices

A comparative study of OFS-heavy vs. expansion-focused IPOs in the Indian market (2024)

Sahyadri College of Engineering & Management · Visvesvaraya Technological University · Guided by Asst. Prof. Ramesh K G

Read the full report (PDF)

The question

When an Indian company lists, the money can go one of two ways: to existing shareholders cashing out (Offer for Sale), or into the business itself (expansion-focused fresh issue). Does that choice change how the stock actually performs on listing day — and how much risk investors take on?

Global research covers IPO underpricing well, but almost nothing compares these two allocation strategies in the Indian market. That gap is what this study set out to close.

What I did

I built a dataset of 330 mainboard IPOs listed on the NSE and BSE through 2024, pulling from RHP prospectuses, SEBI filings, Chittorgarh, Moneycontrol, Screener and Yahoo Finance — then classified every issue as OFS-heavy or expansion-focused and tracked subscription, grey market premium, listing gain and beta for each.

On that base I ran a CAPM framework to separate expected from actual returns, alpha–beta analysis for excess performance, independent-sample t-tests for significance, a decision tree to rank what actually drives listing day returns, plus regression and correlation analysis. Built in Excel, Python, SPSS and Power BI.

What I found

  • Expansion IPOs were subscribed 4× harder — 214.59× average versus 53.68× for OFS-heavy issues.
  • And they listed better — 52.41% average listing day return against 27.47%.
  • They also carried more risk — beta of 0.418 versus 0.328, a clean risk–return tradeoff.
  • Alpha favoured growth — 43.21% excess return versus 18.70%, meaning expansion issues beat what CAPM predicted.
  • Grey market hype barely predicted anything — GMP-to-listing-return correlation of just 0.081, while beta-to-return ran 0.697.

The takeaway: how a company allocates IPO proceeds is a real signal, not a formality. Investors price growth-directed capital more aggressively, and CAPM alone doesn't explain Indian IPO listing behaviour — demand and sentiment do more of the work.
